# Purpose of MEMS

Microgrid Energy Management System (MEMS) is the operational brain of a modern microgrid. It connects generation, storage, consumption, and grid interaction into one coordinated control and monitoring structure so that the system can be operated safely, reliably, and efficiently.

In a microgrid, several energy resources may be active at the same time, such as photovoltaic generation, battery energy storage, loads, EV charging, protection devices, and grid exchange. A microgrid without a dedicated energy management layer is difficult to run in an optimal way because operators must manually balance energy flows, protect critical loads, and react to changing conditions in real time. WAGO MEMS provides this missing layer by collecting data, evaluating the current state of the microgrid, and applying control strategies based on both technical constraints and operational goals.

## Goals of MEMS

The main purpose of MEMS is to ensure that the microgrid behaves as a unified and controllable energy system rather than as a collection of separate devices.

- Reliable operation: maintain stable microgrid behavior in both grid-connected and islanded modes, protect critical loads, and reduce the risk of outages or equipment damage.
- Optimal energy utilization: prioritize renewable generation, reduce fuel consumption, minimize unnecessary grid import, and increase local self-sufficiency when beneficial.
- Storage coordination: manage battery charge and discharge according to state of charge, operating limits, reserve requirements, and current energy demand.
- Source and load balancing: distribute power between generators, storage units, and loads while respecting safety constraints, interlocks, and operating priorities.
- Economic optimization: support peak shaving, demand management, tariff-aware operation, and efficient use of available generation resources.
- Visibility and traceability: provide a clear view of power flow, alarms, trend data, and operating history so that operators can understand what is happening and why.
- Centralized supervision: enable operators to monitor and manage the microgrid from a unified platform while still allowing local autonomy when needed.

## Why MEMS matters

Microgrids are usually more complex than a single installation or a conventional power system. They combine variable renewable power, flexible storage, switching logic, protection functions, and sometimes multiple operating modes. In such an environment, decisions must be made in real time. MEMS provides the logic to interpret system status, detect abnormal conditions, and trigger the correct control action.

This is especially important for:

- maintaining continuity of supply for critical equipment
- reducing energy cost through smarter dispatch and load management
- increasing the use of renewable electricity
- improving resilience during grid disturbances or outages
- providing a clear operational picture for remote and centralized supervision

## MEMS in the VC Hub environment

The MEMS functionality is integrated into the VC Hub platform so that microgrid operation can be managed together with data acquisition, dashboards, alarms, historical storage, reporting, and system visualization. In practice, this means MEMS is not isolated from the rest of the industrial automation environment; it becomes part of a larger digital platform for monitoring, control, and optimization.

The VC Hub-based MEMS approach brings together:

- real-time monitoring of plant and microgrid status
- control of power devices, storage units, and sources
- automation logic for dispatch and switching
- alarm and event handling for abnormal conditions
- historical analysis and reporting for performance review
- integration with other field systems and industrial assets

## Summary

The purpose of MEMS is to make a microgrid controllable, transparent, and efficient. By coordinating generation, storage, and demand, and by applying logic for safe and economical energy management, MEMS helps operators achieve reliable power supply, better renewable utilization, reduced cost, and improved resilience in both normal and abnormal operating conditions.

This manual describes how MEMS is represented, configured, and used within the VC Hub environment so that operators and engineers can understand the purpose, functions, and value of the system in daily microgrid operation.
